Our vision, a complex interplay of physiological processes, can be influenced by various health conditions, including brain tumours. As the central command centre of our body, the brain governs every aspect of our being, including vision. In this blog, we will explore the intricate relationship between brain tumours and vision, examining how these tumours affect sight, the symptoms they may cause, and the implications for diagnosis and treatment.

#### Understanding the Anatomy

To understand how brain tumours impact vision, it's essential to grasp the anatomy involved. The optic nerves, responsible for transmitting visual information from the eyes to the brain, play a crucial role. These nerves converge at the optic chiasm, where signals from both eyes intermingle before proceeding to the brain's visual processing centres. Any disruption along this pathway can lead to visual disturbances.

#### Impact on Optic Nerves

Brain tumours near the optic nerves or optic chiasm can exert pressure on these delicate structures, resulting in various visual impairments. Compression of the optic nerves may lead to decreased visual acuity, blurriness, or even total vision loss in severe cases. Additionally, tumours can impede the transmission of visual signals, causing visual field defects where specific areas of the visual field become obscured or distorted.

#### Types of Visual Disturbances

The nature and severity of visual symptoms can vary depending on the location, size, and type of brain tumour. Tumours situated near the optic nerves or chiasm often manifest as bilateral visual deficits, affecting both eyes. Conversely, tumours affecting specific regions of the brain responsible for visual processing may produce unilateral symptoms or peculiar visual phenomena, such as hallucinations or altered perception of colours and shapes.

#### Common Symptoms

Brain tumour symptoms are crucial for early detection and intervention. Some common indicators include blurred or double vision, peripheral vision loss, visual field defects (e.g., tunnel vision), photopsia (flashes of light), diplopia (double vision), changes in colour perception, and visual hallucinations.

#### Diagnostic Approaches

Diagnosing brain tumours that affect vision requires a multidisciplinary approach combining neuroimaging techniques with ophthalmological assessments. Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) is the gold standard for visualising brain structures and detecting tumours. Additionally, visual field testing, optical coherence tomography (OCT), and fundoscopic examination help evaluate the extent of visual impairment and monitor disease progression.

#### Treatment Considerations

The management of brain tumours impacting vision depends on several factors, including tumour characteristics, location, and the patient's overall health. Brain tumour Treatment modalities may include surgical resection, radiation therapy, chemotherapy, or a combination thereof. The primary goal is to alleviate symptoms, preserve neurological function, and mitigate the risk of tumour recurrence.

#### Surgical Intervention

For accessible tumours that pose a significant risk to vision or overall brain function, surgical removal is often the preferred course of action. Neurosurgeons employ advanced techniques, such as stereotactic navigation and intraoperative neurophysiological monitoring, to precisely target and excise tumours while minimising damage to surrounding structures.

#### Radiation Therapy

In cases where complete tumour resection is unfeasible or carries substantial risks, radiation therapy serves as a valuable adjunct or primary treatment modality. Techniques like stereotactic radiosurgery deliver highly focused radiation beams to the tumour site, effectively halting tumour growth and preserving surrounding neural pathways.

#### Chemotherapy

Chemotherapeutic agents are often employed in the management of malignant brain tumours to impede tumour proliferation and enhance treatment efficacy. However, their utility in addressing visual symptoms directly is limited, and systemic side effects may pose challenges to patients' overall well-being.

### Conclusion

In the intricate connection between vision and brain function, brain tumours can disrupt this delicate balance, affecting our sight. However, advancements in medical science, along with the dedication of healthcare professionals and the resilience of the human spirit, offer hope. By unravelling the mysteries of how brain tumours affect vision, we move towards a brighter future, where sight signifies not just biology but also hope and possibility.
