Failed Back Surgery Syndrome (FBSS) is a condition that affects numerous individuals who have sought back surgery to alleviate chronic pain but have not found the relief they hoped for. Back pain troubles almost one in 10 people worldwide, making it a major cause of disability. When the pain becomes unbearable, some turn to surgery for help. But in some cases, even after surgery, the pain doesn't go away. In this blog, we will examine the complexities of FBSS, look into potential causes, and talk about potential remedies. If you have FBSS, you can learn how to contact a neurosurgeon in Mumbai who can help you manage this condition.

## **What is Failed Back Surgery Syndrome (FBSS)?**

After having back surgery, some people develop the condition known as [Failed Back Surgery Syndrome (FBSS)](https://drjayeshsardhara.com/failed-back-surgery-syndrome/), which causes them to still feel pain. While it's called "failed," it doesn't necessarily mean the surgery was entirely unsuccessful; rather, it highlights that the expected [pain relief](https://drjayeshsardhara.com/the-path-to-pain-free-living/) didn't occur as anticipated.

### **What leads to the occurrence of FBSS?**

Following are some of the reasons which can lead to FBSS: \
**1. Incomplete Decompression:** Sometimes, surgery may not completely address the root cause of pain, resulting in lingering nerve compression or irritation.\
**2. Scar Tissue Formation:** Following surgery, the body's natural reaction is to create scar tissue, a process that can occasionally lead to the entrapment of nerves, subsequently triggering discomfort and pain.\
**3. Incorrect Diagnosis:** In certain situations, an initial [diagnosis](https://drjayeshsardhara.com/navigating-the-diagnosis-what-to-expect-with-a-brain-tumour/) might prove to be inaccurate, resulting in surgical procedures that do not effectively target the actual origin of the pain.\
**4. Hardware Complications:** Surgical hardware, like screws or plates, can occasionally induce discomfort or necessitate removal due to complications or patient-specific factors.\
**5. Psychological Factors:** Pain perception is not solely physical; it can also be influenced by psychological factors, and these psychological aspects can indeed have an impact on the development and persistence of FBSS.

### **What are the treatment options available for FBSS?**

Let’s take a look at the possible treatment options for FBSS: \
**1. Conservative approaches:** These include options like physical therapy, pain management, and the use of medications to alleviate discomfort when dealing with FBSS.\
**2. Revision Surgery:**  In some cases, a second surgical procedure may be necessary to correct issues from the initial surgery, which could involve minimally invasive techniques like [endoscopic spine surgery](https://drjayeshsardhara.com/the-path-to-pain-free-living/) or [endoscopic back surgery](https://drjayeshsardhara.com/failed-back-surgery-syndrome/).\
**3. Epidural Steroid Injections:** These injections deliver anti-inflammatory medication directly to the site of discomfort, offering temporary relief from pain associated with Failed Back Surgery Syndrome.\
**4. Spinal Cord Stimulation (SCS):** This implantable specialized device sends controlled electrical signals that effectively mask or override pain signals, offering relief to select patients dealing with Failed Back Surgery Syndrome (FBSS).\
**5. Physical Rehabilitation:** Tailored physical therapy programs aim to enhance strength, flexibility, and mobility, catering to the unique requirements of FBSS patients.\
**6. Alternative Therapies:**  Complementary treatments such as acupuncture, chiropractic care, and biofeedback have shown promise in providing relief to certain individuals dealing with FBSS.\
