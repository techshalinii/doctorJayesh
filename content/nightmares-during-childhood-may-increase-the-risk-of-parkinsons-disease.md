If your child has been waking up due to nightmares, it may raise the risk of Parkinson’s Disease. Parkinson’s Disease (PD) is a progressive disorder caused by degeneration of nerve cells leading to disruption in balance and coordination, tremors (trembling in the hands, arms, legs, and jaw), rigidity (stiffness), and slow movement. A recent study by Abidemi I.

Otaiku conducted at Birmingham City Hospital has identified that having frequent distressing dreams (bad dreams and nightmares) during childhood, may be an early sign or potentially modifiable risk factor for developing both Dementia and PD. In this study, a total sample size of 6991 children was analyzed by assessing the presence of distressing dreams when the children were 7 and 11 years of age. The study stated that as compared to children who did not experience distressing dreams, those children who had persistent distressing dreams were 85% more likely to develop cognitive impairment or PD by age of 50.

**[Dr. Jayesh Sardhara](https://drjayeshsardhara.com/)**, Senior Consultant, Neuro & Spine Surgery, Fortis Hospital, Mulund says, “There is a possibility that in some individuals distressing dreams are an early manifestation of age-related neurodegenerative diseases such as PD, Dementia or Alzheimer’s Disease (AD). These diseases occur as a result of neurodegeneration in the right frontal brain regions, which are required for down regulating negative emotions during Rapid Eye Movement (REM) sleep.”

How parents can keep an eye on the signs According to Dr. Sardhara, “Bad dreams or Nightmares can hamper a child’s sleep and cause them to wake up feeling scared and upset. It is normal for children to have bad dreams about:

1) Realistic dangers like aggressive dogs, sharks, or spiders

2) Imaginary fears like monsters

3) Distressing events they’ve seen or experienced

4) Nightmares tend to occur in the second half of the night and are common in children of all ages, especially in children aged 10 years.”

### **Tips for dealing with nightmares:**

As a parent it is often important to recognize such events and at the same time comfort the child. We advocate “CARESS HIM” technique to the parents:

**C** - Calmly talking about the bad dream can help in reducing its emotional power

**A** - Avoid dismissing the fear they experienced as nightmares can seem real to them

**R** - Reassure your child that everything is ok and that they’re safe

**E** - Explain to the child it was a bad dream

**SS** - Make sure your child gets Sound Sleep

**H** - Hug, cuddle and a kiss might help alleviate fear

**I**- Introspect regarding daily interactions with other children, television shows, computer games or other daytime experiences. Avoiding these triggers may help avoid nightmares

**M**- Consult a Medical professional if your child is repeatedly having nightmares, which is causing Anxiety during the day

“To conclude, with increasing evidence that raises the possibility that distressing dreams may be an independent risk factor for neurodegeneration, it may be possible that timely treatment of distressing dreams would become a primary prevention strategy for Dementia and PD. However, further studies are needed to confirm this association to be casual,” Dr. Sardhara.
